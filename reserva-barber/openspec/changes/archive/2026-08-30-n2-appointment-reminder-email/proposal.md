# N2 — The appointment reminder, and the idempotency N1 got for free

## Why

**A confirmed appointment is a promise made once, at the moment money moved, and then never mentioned again.** A client who books on Tuesday for Saturday receives one message on Tuesday and hears nothing until Saturday. Between those two points the product has no voice at all: no page they will revisit, no notification, nothing. The only artifact carrying the appointment is an email four days up their inbox.

The cost of that silence is not the forgotten haircut. **It is the slot that dies unsold.** A client who realises on Friday night that they cannot come has, today, exactly one path — find the confirmation email, scroll to the link, open the page, cancel — and the likelihood of anyone walking that path unprompted is the likelihood of them thinking about a barbershop appointment on a Friday night. So they do not cancel. They no-show, the shop stands empty at 15:00 (T77's phrasing, and this is the other end of the same problem), and the deposit is the only thing the shop keeps from a slot it could have resold with a day's notice.

**A reminder is therefore not a courtesy message. It is the product's only mechanism for recovering a slot from a client who has changed their mind**, and its value is concentrated entirely in the cancellation link it carries — which C1 built, and which nothing since has put back in front of a client at the moment they would use it.

Three things make now the moment:

- **Every part is already built and proven.** N1 has the port, the adapter, the escaping rules, the origin resolution, the failure semantics and the copy structure. B7 has the scheduled Worker, its deploy config, its secret, its batch bounds and its summary contract. C1 has the page the link opens. **This story writes a candidate query, a claim, a builder and a job — and nothing else is new.**
- **The roadmap's dependency line for N2 is wrong in a way worth correcting in writing rather than in code.** It reads _"depends on: N1, B7 (reuses the Cron Trigger)"_, which implies two things that are false. It does not reuse N1's at-most-once guarantee, because that guarantee is a property of a guarded status transition and **a reminder has no transition to key on**. And it does not reuse "the Cron Trigger" in the sense of adding a handler to the application's Worker — `worker/sweep.ts` records the measurement that forbids exactly that (3812 KiB gzip against the free plan's then-current 3072 KiB ceiling, the Prisma query compiler bundled twice). T51 has since closed and the ceiling is 10 MiB, but its closing note keeps that split standing on its own terms: bundling the query compiler twice was a correctness problem, not a size one. What N2 reuses is the **`reserva-barber-cron` Worker**.
- **T76 blocks the runtime verification and will keep blocking it, so the design must be shaped for that rather than around it.** No sending domain is verified, `RESEND_API_KEY` is deliberately unset in production, and this story cannot change that. It can, and must, refuse to lie about it: no page may promise a reminder, and the story may not report itself delivered end to end.

## What Changes

- **A reminder email is sent once, a fixed lead time before a confirmed appointment, by a scheduled job.** It carries the same appointment, branch, barber, service and money the confirmation carried, and the same link to the client's own booking page — this time with the cancellation named as the reason to follow it, because that is now the point of the message rather than a secondary use of it.

- **`Booking` gains `reminderEmailSentAt`, and it is an idempotency key — which is the exact opposite of what `confirmationEmailSentAt` is, a few lines above it in the same model.** N1's column carries a doc comment stating in bold that it is _not_ an idempotency key and that nothing reads it before sending, because at-most-once delivery is a property of the confirming conditional update. **N2 has no such update.** Its trigger is time passing, and nothing in the database changes to mark a booking as due. So the claim _is_ the write: one statement selects and marks, guarded on the column being null and the status being `CONFIRMED`, and a second run matches zero rows. Two columns with opposite semantics on one table is a genuine hazard, and the schema comment must say so explicitly rather than leave the next reader to apply N1's rule to N2's column.

- **The claim happens before the send, deliberately inverting N1's ordering.** N1 records after the provider accepts, because a failed send there must leave a queryable "this client was never told". Doing that here produces a mail loop: a Worker that dies between send and record, or a provider that accepts and then times out, leaves the row unclaimed, and the next run re-sends. A Monday 10:00 appointment would collect one reminder per invocation for a day. **Claim-first bounds the failure to one client losing one reminder — recoverable by looking, and still a `WHERE` clause. Send-first bounds nothing.**

- **The candidate rule is a window that ends at the appointment, not a window around a target instant.** `status = 'CONFIRMED' AND reminderEmailSentAt IS NULL AND startTime > now AND startTime < now + LEAD`. This is **self-healing**: a failed run, a Worker outage, a deploy — each is caught up by the next tick with no window arithmetic tied to the cron cadence, and no reconciliation. It also makes the single most dangerous defect in this story impossible by construction, which is the next point.

- **`startTime > now` is a safety bound, not a filter.** Without it, the first production run reminds **every confirmed booking the database has ever held**, including everything the gate scripts and every earlier story left behind. That is B7's unbounded-first-run warning with the blast radius moved from the pooler to real inboxes, and it is unrecoverable. It is stated as a requirement rather than left implicit in a `WHERE` clause.

- **A booking made inside the lead window is never reminded of itself.** Someone booking at 08:00 for 09:30 would otherwise receive a "reminder" minutes after the confirmation email that carried the same appointment and the same link. Suppressed by comparing `startTime` against `createdAt` — deliberately not `updatedAt`, which `markConfirmationEmailSent` bumps on every confirmed booking, so it is not the booking's age.

- **The lead time is a declared constant, disclosed as a judgement no real shop has measured.** `EXPIRY_GRACE_MINUTES` carries exactly that disclosure and is the precedent. T78's rule — do not invent shop policy silently — applies with one difference that must be named: a grace of zero is a coherent product, and a lead of zero is not, so a number has to exist here. Making it per-shop configurable is out of scope for the same reason T78 gives about the surface no owner has ever expressed a policy on.

- **`createEmailSender` stops depending on `process.env`, and this is the defect most likely to ship silently.** It reads `process.env[RESEND_API_KEY]` today. A scheduled invocation has no request context — `worker/sweep.ts` documents that bindings arrive on `env`, which is why it imports `createPrismaClient(connectionString)` rather than the memoized `getPrismaClient`. If this is carried over unchanged, both values resolve absent, `UnconfiguredEmailSender` returns `rejected`, **and because the claim is already written every booking is permanently marked as reminded while nobody was reminded.** The feature fails completely on its first run and every surface still looks correct. The factory gains an explicit-configuration entry point; the `process.env` read stays at the Next.js composition roots.

- **A second cron on the existing Worker, dispatched by `event.cron`.** Separate expressions rather than two calls in one handler, because `sweep.ts` rethrows to mark an invocation failed in the platform's own view of the schedule, and a reminder fault must never make a healthy sweep look dead. Hourly rather than five-minutely, because at a lead measured in hours nothing depends on the message landing within five minutes, and 288 daily invocations against a pooler capped at five connections buys nothing.

- **The cron Worker gains `APP_ORIGIN`, and `wrangler.cron.jsonc`'s comment saying it has none is corrected rather than left standing.** That comment is currently true and load-bearing — it explains that the Worker renders nothing and is reachable by nobody. This story falsifies half of it. Rewriting a justification a change invalidates is what B1 did to T33 and T17 and what B6 did to `bookingConfirmationService.ts`; leaving it is how a comment becomes a lie the next reader trusts.

- **A reminder with no usable origin still sends, and the consequence is worse than N1's.** A confirmation without a link is still a receipt. A reminder without a link has had its entire actionable value removed, since the cancellation path _is_ the message's purpose. It sends anyway — a client is still better off knowing — and it logs at error, with the spec stating that the operator consequence is larger here than on the confirmation path.

- **A new builder, not a flag on the existing one.** `bookingConfirmationEmail.ts` is reused as a shape, not as an implementation. Two messages behind one boolean parameter is how they become one confusable message, and the escaping, money and timezone helpers are already shared at the level where sharing is safe.

- **Nothing a client or an owner can see changes.** No page, no component, no counter — B7's precedent. In particular **the confirmation email must not promise a reminder**: with `RESEND_API_KEY` unset in production, that sentence would be false in the one place that cannot be redeployed.

- **A reminder can be sent for an appointment cancelled seconds earlier, and this change bounds that rather than eliminating it.** The claim's status guard closes the read-to-claim race; nothing closes claim-to-send. Small batches and an immediate send keep the window to milliseconds. Stating the residue is the honest form; claiming exactly-once against a concurrent human would not be true.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `booking-reminder-email`: when the reminder is due and what makes a booking a candidate; the claim that provides at-most-once delivery and why it is ordered before the send; the past-appointment and short-notice bounds; what the message carries and how its purpose differs from the confirmation's; the origin dependency and its degraded form; the cross-owner port and its stated exception; the batch bounds, the single run instant, and the summary emitted even when nothing was due; the escaping and header rules inherited from the confirmation path; and the deliverability prerequisite this story cannot satisfy.

### Modified Capabilities

- `booking-hold-expiration`: the sweep stops being the only thing its Worker does. Its requirement that every invocation emits a summary — the requirement the capability's honesty rests on — now needs the co-tenant statement: a reminder fault SHALL NOT mark the sweep's invocation failed, and the sweep's rethrow SHALL NOT suppress the reminder's own summary. The five-minute cadence is unchanged and stays a data-freshness choice.
- `data-persistence`: `Booking` gains `reminderEmailSentAt` with semantics opposite to the column beside it, a partial index Prisma cannot declare (`(startTime) WHERE status = 'CONFIRMED' AND "reminderEmailSentAt" IS NULL`, following `b7_expired_hold_indexes`), and a second explicit email projection — the second booking read in this product that deliberately selects the client's email address, and the first that does so without an owner scope.
- `cloudflare-deployment`: the cron Worker gains a second cron trigger, two non-secret vars (`APP_ORIGIN`, `EMAIL_FROM`) and one secret (`RESEND_API_KEY`) that until now belonged to the application Worker alone — the same value in two places, uploaded as exact bytes by `secret bulk` from a BOM-free JSON file, which is the hour B7 lost twice to the interactive prompt.
- `booking-confirmation-email`: no behaviour changes, but the capability's configuration requirement is written as though one kind of composition root exists. It gains the statement that the sender factory admits configuration explicitly for callers with no request context, and that the `process.env` read is a Next.js-only convenience rather than the contract.

## Impact

**Schema** — one nullable column, one migration, one partial index in raw SQL. `Booking.reminderEmailSentAt DateTime? @db.Timestamptz(3)`, zone-aware at creation per the convention the model states at `startTime`. The index is added in the migration and named in the model's existing "INDEXES PRISMA CANNOT DECLARE" comment block; the plan is confirmed with `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` against the live database rather than assumed, per D1 and D7.

**Code** — one new domain builder, one new domain capability constant, two new booking-horizon constants, one new cross-owner port, one application service, one Prisma repository, one factory entry point, one entrypoint dispatch, one copy namespace. `emailSenderFactory.ts` is the only existing file whose behaviour changes; everything else is addition.

**Dependencies** — **none added to `package.json`.** Same reason as N1 and B5: T51, and one endpoint never justifying a vendor SDK.

**Configuration** — `RESEND_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM` and `APP_ORIGIN` on the **cron** Worker (`-c wrangler.cron.jsonc`), plus a second cron expression. The application Worker is unchanged.

**Bundle** — the cron Worker currently imports no copy module, and this chain pulls in `src/lib/copy.ts` (85 KB raw, a single object literal tree-shaking cannot narrow) plus the builder, the Resend adapter and `publicOrigin`. **This was written as a risk and the baseline measurement retired it before implementation began**: the cron Worker is **879.54 KiB gzip** against the paid plan's **10 MiB**, because T51 closed when Franco took Workers Paid before N1. The measurement is still taken after the change — an unexpected jump would mean a page, a route or a React component reached a Worker that must contain none of them — but it is no longer a design input, and the reminder-scoped copy module that was the fallback is not needed.

**Verification** — a live gate script following B7's, since the guarantees are timing, isolation and idempotence against real rows and a mock can certify a job that cannot run. The scheduled trigger is fired by hand (`wrangler dev -c wrangler.cron.jsonc --test-scheduled`) because no unit test executes the entrypoint.

**Blocked, and recorded rather than waived** — T76 stands unchanged: no verified sending domain, so production cannot send and must not be given the key. **N2 is strictly worse off than N1 here.** A confirmation that fails to send still has the booking page's could-not-send state telling the client the truth. A reminder that fails to send has **no surface at all** — the client's experience of a broken reminder and of a working one are byte-identical. This story closes with that gap named, not with a green suite reported as delivery.

**Constraints to respect** — **T76** (above), **T71** (a reminder roughly doubles mail volume and arrives as a burst rather than spread across the day, so the most likely production shape is reminders exhausting the cap and every _confirmation_ after it being throttled — the message that carries no money starving the one that does), **T51** (the ceiling), **T56** (a third place holding guest personal data, still with no deletion path), and **T57** (the capability argument has no default, which is the hole the cancellation notice fell through).

**Out of scope, named rather than discovered** — rescheduling (no such feature exists), SMS or WhatsApp (excluded by `base-standards.md` §4), a second reminder at a shorter lead, a per-shop configurable lead time (T78's reasoning), an owner-facing "who has not been reminded" surface (T70/T77's recipient class), and any retry of a failed reminder.
